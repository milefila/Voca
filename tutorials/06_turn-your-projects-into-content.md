# Tutorial 06: Turn Your Projects Into Content
**Series:** Design Studio OS | Design Thread Studio
**Runtime:** ~4 minutes
**Format:** Talking head + screen share demo

---

## PRE-PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Tone:** Energizing, celebratory — this is the payoff tutorial. They've done the work, now they learn how to amplify it.
- **Screen share:** Show an actual (or mock) project photo, then Claude generating multiple content variations from it.
- **B-roll:** A beautifully finished room, behind-the-scenes shots on a project site, someone looking at their Instagram insights.
- **Closing message:** This tutorial wraps the series, so end with a clear invitation to the next step (whatever your CTA is — joining your community, booking a session, downloading a toolkit).

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN ON: B-roll of a completed design reveal — a gorgeous room coming together. Cut to camera.]**

You just finished a project. There's a beautiful space sitting in your camera roll and in your clients' home.

And then what happens?

For most designers: you post a few photos, write a caption on the fly, maybe do a carousel — and then nothing. Until the next project is done. Which might be weeks or months from now.

**[Camera.]**

Here's the thing. That one project has enough content to sustain a month of consistent posting. The behind-the-scenes photos. The before and afters. The process. The sourcing decisions. The design decisions you almost made but didn't. The challenges that no one saw but that made the outcome possible.

AI helps you extract and articulate all of that — fast.

**[TRANSITION TO SCREEN SHARE: Claude.ai]**

I'm going to show you the Content Extraction Prompt I use immediately after every project wraps.

**[SHOW PROMPT ON SCREEN. Read it fully.]**

> *"I just completed an interior design project. Here are the details: [write 3-5 sentences describing the space, the client's goals, the design direction, and the outcome]. Based on this, generate a content plan with the following: five Instagram caption options of different tones (storytelling, educational, aspirational, personal reflection, and behind-the-scenes), three short-form video script hooks for Reels or TikTok, two LinkedIn post options that speak to my design process and professional insight, and one email newsletter paragraph that shares a lesson or insight from this project. Include relevant hashtag sets for the Instagram posts."*

**[Paste in a project description and generate.]**

**[CUT TO: Output loading. Scroll through slowly.]**

Look at what comes back.

Five Instagram captions — completely different in tone and angle. Same project. Same photos. Five different ways to talk about it to five different segments of your audience.

**[Highlight the storytelling caption.]**

This one leads with the client's emotional goal — the "calm but not boring" living room. That's a caption that people save.

**[Scroll to the educational option.]**

This one breaks down the sourcing decision for the main sofa — why you chose it, what problem it solved. That's a caption that gets shared.

**[Scroll to the Reels hooks.]**

Three hooks for short-form video. You don't have to use all of them — but now you have a starting point. You can record any of these as a thirty-second video in your car on the way home from the reveal.

**[Back to camera.]**

Here's the workflow I'd recommend: immediately after a project, before you've lost the details, open Claude and run this prompt. Spend twenty minutes editing and personalizing the output. Then batch-schedule everything for the next four weeks.

You've gone from one project to a month of content in under an hour.

**[Pause. A little more personal here.]**

I want to say something about the social media piece specifically, because I know a lot of designers feel conflicted about it.

Posting consistently is not vanity. It's visibility. It's how the next client who is right for your work finds you. And when you're running a boutique studio and doing the work and managing the relationships — you don't have leftover hours to write captions.

This is where AI being your junior assistant is the most immediately useful. You are still deciding *what* to share and *how* your brand sounds. You're just not typing it from scratch at eleven o'clock at night.

**[Direct to camera, warm close.]**

This is the last tutorial in the Design Studio OS series — and I hope what you're walking away with isn't just a set of tools. I hope it's a shift in how you think about your time.

The creative work is irreplaceable. The systems around it don't have to be.

Every hour you reclaim from writing briefs and proposals and emails and captions is an hour you give back to the thing you actually became a designer to do.

**[Small smile. Unhurried.]**

If you want to go deeper — if you want the templates, the full prompt library, and the workflow documentation behind the Design Studio OS — the link is in the description.

Thank you for being here. I'll see you in the next one.
