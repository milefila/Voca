# Tutorial 02: The AI Client Intake System
**Series:** Design Studio OS | Design Thread Studio
**Runtime:** ~5 minutes
**Format:** Talking head + screen share demo

---

## PRE-PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Tone:** Practical, step-by-step. Like a colleague walking you through something over coffee.
- **Screen share:** Show Tally or Typeform for the intake form, then Claude.ai for the brief generation.
- **On-screen overlays:** Highlight the exact prompt text as it appears on screen.
- **B-roll:** Shots of a discovery call, a cluttered inbox, checking a phone — to set up the problem.

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN ON: B-roll of a crowded calendar, then cut to camera.]**

Here's something I used to do a lot.

A potential client would reach out, I'd get on a 45-minute discovery call to understand their project, take a bunch of notes, hang up, and then spend another hour trying to turn those notes into something coherent I could actually work from.

Two hours minimum. Before I'd even started the project.

**[Camera, direct.]**

Now? I spend about eight minutes on that same call. Because before it ever happens, my AI intake system has already collected everything I need — and generated a first-draft creative brief.

Let me show you exactly how to build this.

**[TRANSITION TO SCREEN SHARE: Tally.so or Typeform form builder]**

Step one is your intake form. I use Tally — it's free and clean — but you could use Typeform or even a Google Form. The point isn't the tool, it's the questions.

You want to collect:

- What kind of space is this? *(living room, primary suite, full-home, commercial)*
- What's the budget range?
- Describe your current space in three words.
- Describe your dream space in three words.
- What's not working right now?
- Who lives or works in this space, and how do they use it?
- Are there any non-negotiables — things you absolutely need or absolutely cannot have?
- Do you have any inspiration images saved anywhere?

**[Point to the form on screen.]**

That last question about inspiration images — I always ask them to upload or link images before we ever speak. It tells me more about a client than they can articulate in words, and it saves both of us from a lot of guesswork.

**[TRANSITION TO: Claude.ai, new conversation open]**

Once a client fills this in — and I send this form the moment someone expresses interest, before scheduling a call — I take their answers and paste them into Claude with this prompt.

**[SHOW THE PROMPT ON SCREEN. Read it aloud slowly.]**

> *"You are a boutique interior design assistant helping prepare for a client discovery call. Below are the answers a prospective client submitted in a pre-consultation intake form. Based on their responses, write a structured design brief that includes: a one-paragraph project summary, the client's stated needs and constraints, their aesthetic direction based on their words and image descriptions, and three smart questions I should ask during our discovery call to fill in any gaps. Write in a professional but warm tone."*

Then I paste in everything from the form and hit send.

**[CUT TO: The Claude output appearing on screen. Let it generate in real time if possible.]**

And here's what comes back.

**[Scroll through the output slowly. Highlight with your cursor as you read.]**

A project summary. Needs and constraints. Aesthetic direction — it picks up the emotional language the client used and reflects it back. And three questions I should actually ask them.

**[Back to camera.]**

This means by the time I get on that discovery call, I've already read a brief about their project. I know their words. I know their constraints. I'm not starting from zero — I'm refining.

And if the call confirms everything in the brief, I've saved myself an hour of post-call admin work.

**[Pause.]**

Now I want to address something. Some designers feel strange about using AI during intake — like they're cutting corners on the relationship. I want to gently push back on that.

The intake form and the brief don't replace your relationship. They *protect* the time you spend in the actual relationship. When you show up to a discovery call already knowing what a client cares about, they feel heard before you've said a word. That's not a shortcut. That's a better experience.

**[Direct to camera.]**

Your action step for today: build your intake form with those eight questions and send it to your next lead before you schedule a call. That's it. You don't have to use AI today — just start collecting the data. The brief generation comes after.

Next, we're talking about concept development — specifically how to move from brief to mood board direction without spending a full afternoon on Pinterest.

See you in Tutorial 03.
