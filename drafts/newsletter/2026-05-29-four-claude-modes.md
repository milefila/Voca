# I use all 4 Claude modes. Most designers don't know 3 exist.

**Subject:** 4 Claude modes (and which one I use when)
**Preview text:** Most designers are paying for all of this and only using the chat box.

---

Most designers open Claude, type something into the chat box, and close it.

I did that for months. Then I looked at what else was sitting in my subscription — and realized I'd been using maybe 20% of what I was paying for.

There are four distinct Claude modes. I use all of them. They do different things. Here's the map.

---

## 1. Claude Chat + Projects — The project brain

This is the one everyone knows. But most people use it like a search engine — one question, one answer, done.

I use it differently. Every active project has its own Claude Project. The moment a client signs, I create a Project and load it: the brief, the scope, the client's style references, their budget range. Claude already knows the job before I type a word.

That changes everything. I use it in contractor meetings to pull specs on the fly. I drop in furniture images and ask whether they work with the palette we've committed to. I use it for sourcing — describe the piece I need, the price point, the lead time constraints, and get options. I use it to draft specs, write contractor follow-ups, and think through layouts.

The difference between Claude Chat and Claude Chat with Projects is the difference between a smart assistant and one who actually knows your clients.

**Try this:** Create a Project for your next active job. Load the brief and a few inspiration images before your next contractor call.

---

## 2. Claude Cowork — The business engine

This is the agentic version of Claude. You give it a goal and it executes — not just answers.

I use Cowork for the business side of my practice. Marketing, content, emails, proposals. The kind of work that has to get done but keeps getting pushed to the end of the day when my brain is gone.

I don't prompt it line by line. I tell it what I need — "draft a welcome email sequence for new inquiry leads, three emails, warm but professional, here's how I work and what makes my studio different" — and I come back to a finished draft. Not a starting point. A draft.

For social content, same thing. I give it a few recent projects, my tone, what I want readers to walk away thinking, and it batches content I can actually use.

The rule I've landed on: if the task involves more than one step and touches real documents or content, Cowork handles it better than Chat.

**Try this:** Write out one recurring task you do every month — a client update, a content batch, a proposal template. Hand it to Cowork as a goal, not a question.

---

## 3. Claude Design — The client-facing one

This is the one designers aren't talking about enough.

Claude Design lives at claude.ai/design. You describe what you want — a pitch deck, a client presentation, a project one-pager — and it builds a first draft. Not a blank slide with your cursor blinking at you. A structured, designed first draft.

I use it for pitch decks when I'm going after a new project. I use it for client presentations at design reveal. I use it for one-pagers when a client needs something to share with their spouse or their contractor.

What changed for me: I stopped treating proposal and presentation prep as a half-day task. I describe the project, the client, the key beats I need to hit, and I get a working structure back in minutes. I'm editing and adding my photography from there — not starting from scratch.

If you've been opening Canva, staring at a blank page, and then spending two hours on formatting instead of content, this is the tab to open next.

**Try this:** Take your next client presentation and describe it to Claude Design before you open any other tool. Let it build the skeleton.

---

## 4. Claude Code — The unexpected one

This is the one no one expects an interior designer to mention.

Claude Code is terminal-based. It runs deep, long tasks on your computer. It was built for developers. It is also, if you're willing to experiment, genuinely useful for designers who want to build things that don't exist yet.

I've been vibe coding with it. I've built apps — tools I wanted that I couldn't find, things that solve specific problems in my workflow. You don't write the code yourself. You describe what you want, Claude builds it, and you iterate by telling it what to change.

Is this for everyone? No. You have to be willing to sit with some discomfort while you figure out how it works. But if there's a tool you've always wished existed for your practice — a client-facing budget tracker, a FF&E checklist generator, something specific to how you work — this is how you build it without hiring a developer.

**Try this:** Think of one tool you wish existed for your practice. Open Claude Code and describe it. See what comes back.

---

## The point

You don't need more AI tools.

You need to use the ones you're already paying for.

If you're on Claude Pro, all of this is in your subscription. Most designers are living in the chat box and leaving three other modes completely untouched.

The designers moving fastest right now aren't using more software. They know which Claude to open for which job.

Chat for the project brain. Cowork for the business work. Design for the client-facing deliverables. Code for the tools that don't exist yet.

Pick one tab you haven't opened and try it this week. That's it.
